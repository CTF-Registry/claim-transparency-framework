# CTF Entry Point (A) — Non-Verifiable Claims & Guidance Content

## What problem this addresses
A growing share of online content contains claims that are difficult or impossible to verify (e.g., guidance, spiritual assertions, intuitive readings, predictive statements, or other non-verifiable propositions).  
The core issue is not whether such claims are “true” or “false”, but the lack of consistent, structured disclosure about what is being asserted and what is being promised.

## What CTF provides
CTF (Claim Transparency Framework) is a neutral, declarative standard that enables a structured claim disclosure without:
- judging truth,
- validating outcomes,
- certifying practitioners,
- or arbitrating disputes.

CTF focuses on **what is declared**: claim type, claim level, scope, reproducibility stance, and explicit promise/non-promise flags.

## Who this is useful for
CTF is designed to be usable by:
- platforms (trust & safety / policy / compliance),
- marketplaces,
- professional directories,
- and any environment where non-verifiable claims can create consumer confusion or dispute risk.

## What CTF does NOT do
CTF does not remove content, does not label anyone as legitimate or illegitimate, and does not provide legal protection by itself.  
It is a transparency layer intended to reduce ambiguity and improve interpretability.
